S&P 500  ·  Nasdaq-100  ·  Updated each trading day

Each trading day, S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 technology stocks with a new product on the news: a news headline from the last 3 trading days that starts with the company's name followed by a launch word, such as "Apple unveils…", "Nvidia launches…" or "Microsoft introduces…". Stocks are ranked by how many news outlets carried the launch, then by trading volume compared with its 21-day average, up to ten. Only stocks in the scanner's Technology sector are listed, so some days have fewer than ten. Headlines come from Google News and are matched automatically, so some launches are missed and a few headlines that are not product launches slip through. The list does not judge whether the product is any good.

There is too little history to backtest this properly: the headline archive starts in mid 2026. Over 38 trading days (August to September 2026), the technology-only rule averaged +2.0% per 1-month list vs SPY +0.2%, ahead of SPY in 63% of lists, with about six stocks a list. The same rule across all sectors averaged −0.8%. Lists that far apart overlap heavily, so this is only about two independent months. That sample is far too small to judge. Past results do not guarantee future results.

Returns are price only, from the list date's close to the close 21 trading days (about 1 month) later, adjusted for splits, before dividends, taxes and trading costs. Lists younger than 21 trading days show their return so far, marked "day N of 21", and are left out of the summary until they finish. "List mean" is the average of the listed stocks' returns (up to ten); SPY is the S&P 500 ETF over the same span. Lists marked † were rebuilt on October 25, 2026 with the current rule; lists from October 26, 2026 on are recorded each evening.
